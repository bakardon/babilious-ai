# Why Hire Abubakar?

If I were attempting to recruit expedition members—and fortunately for everyone, I am not—I would at least consider Abubakar for three reasons.

## 1. He Learns Through Difficulty

Many people enjoy success.

Many people enjoy learning.

Far fewer enjoy learning when success is not guaranteed.

Over the years, Abubakar has repeatedly taken on projects that forced him into unfamiliar territory: machine learning, medical imaging, networking systems, data science, and software development. His first instinct when encountering a difficult problem is usually not to avoid it, but to understand it.

This tendency occasionally creates additional work for him.

It also creates growth.

## 2. He Combines Technical Curiosity With Ambition

Most students complete assignments.

Abubakar tends to turn them into expeditions.

Whether working with fMRI datasets, tumor prognosis prediction systems, networking infrastructure, or AI applications, he is rarely satisfied with simply reaching the finish line. He wants to understand how systems work, how they fail, and how they can be improved.

This curiosity often pushes him beyond course requirements and into independent exploration.

## 3. He Is Still Becoming

The most interesting thing about Abubakar is not what he has already built.

It is what he is trying to become.

He wants to develop both technical competence and emotional intelligence. He studies engineering, but he also pays attention to leadership, communication, entrepreneurship, decision-making, and human behavior.

His long-term goal is not simply to build software.

It is to build meaningful things and lead meaningful work.

## Final Observation

Every explorer carries strengths and weaknesses.

Abubakar's greatest strength is curiosity.

His greatest weakness is also curiosity.

He has a habit of discovering new mountains before finishing the current one.

The upside is that he never stays still for very long.
